# Copy and Structure

## Page argument

Adapt the reference's sequence to the buyer's decision. A robust service-business sequence is:

1. contact/trust header;
2. outcome-led hero with one dominant visual;
3. compact offer CTA;
4. early testimonial proof;
5. proof/reassurance strip;
6. problem-to-after-state contrast;
7. service-fit or mechanism section;
8. project types or completeness;
9. process;
10. differentiated advantages;
11. FAQ;
12. final CTA and supporting proof.

Change the sequence when research shows a different objection order. Avoid adding sections merely to mimic the reference length.

## Copy rules

- One page, one audience, one primary offer, one exact CTA phrase.
- The H1 names the visitor's desired result and matches the traffic/search context.
- A headline should tell skimmers why the section matters, not merely label it.
- Lead with `you/your` outcomes; use `we/our` for mechanism and accountability.
- Name concrete avoided problems: rework, delay, uncertainty, poor fit, hidden scope, or wrong method.
- Explain features only through their consequence for the buyer.
- Repeat CTA wording exactly in hero, mid-page, final CTA, modal submit, and documentation.
- Keep the follow-up promise identical in modal and thank-you page.

## Proof rhythm

- Put one strong proof signal in or immediately after the hero.
- Use several short reviews rather than one long wall of text.
- Highlight the strongest original phrase; never rewrite it into a stronger claim.
- Scatter service-specific proof near the relevant argument.
- Show aggregate counts/ratings only when freshly verified and dated in the claim ledger.

## Copy artifacts

`SECTION-COPY.md` should be implementation-ready and include:

- H1, subheadline, CTA, follow-up promise;
- each section ID, headline, body, bullets/cards, proof, and CTA;
- modal labels/options and thank-you copy;
- brochure cover promise and final download wording.

`COPY-GATE-CHECKLIST.md` should explicitly pass/fail:

- message match;
- CTA consistency;
- proof integrity;
- objection coverage;
- benefit-led headlines;
- source qualifiers;
- page/form/thank-you/catalogue consistency;
- banned invented proof.

Do not start final UI assembly while this gate fails.

## Final clarity pass

For every headline and bullet, identify the specific service/outcome and the source that supports any factual claim. A cold reader should understand the important noun without guessing. Each bullet must support the section headline. Preserve claim qualifiers (what, who, timeframe) and distinguish client outcomes from company achievements. Use supplied keyword evidence when the page targets search traffic; label inferred intent rather than inventing advertising-account data. The reference's persuasive sequence is a guide, not a compulsory universal section count.
