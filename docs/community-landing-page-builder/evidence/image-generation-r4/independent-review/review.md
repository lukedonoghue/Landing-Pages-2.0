# Clarentis R4: Independent Visual and Editorial Review

**Result: blocked for local final.** One reproducible dialog defect obscures the visible close control after validation. This is a read-only review; no page or skill files were changed.

Reviewed the supplied project's `index.html`, `styles.css`, `script.js`, `privacy.html`, four original scene images and their rendered placements, plus the live public source at [clarentis.co.uk](https://clarentis.co.uk/). The requested preview at `127.0.0.1:43877` was unavailable, so the same project was served read-only at `http://127.0.0.1:43911/`. Captures cover 390x844, 768x1024, 1024x800, 1280x600, 1440x900 and a 320x700 first screen. I did not consult the builder's acceptance conclusions, other attempts or audits.

## Prioritized Findings

1. **P1, blocking: validation scroll hides the modal exit.** Empty `Prepare email draft` at 390x844 leaves the dialog scrolled 48px: the close button occupies viewport y=-18..18, with its heading clipped at the top. At 1280x600, dialog scroll is 70px and the close button occupies y=-34..2, effectively invisible. The first invalid field and summary remain visible, so this is specifically a lost visible exit and orientation cue during error recovery. See [mobile validation](mobile-modal-validation.png) and [short-height validation](short-laptop-modal-validation.png). `script.js:63-67,130` centers the invalid field while the entire capped dialog scrolls (`styles.css:166-173`). Keep the title/close control visible while the form body scrolls, then recapture both states.
2. **P2: the first scroll repeats the hero instead of adding a decision aid.** The assurance strip repeats the hero's free consultation, fixed-fee proposal and UK small-business fit almost verbatim ([mobile first screen](mobile-first.png), [desktop first screen](desktop-first.png); `index.html:48-65`). The [public source first screen](source-desktop-first.png) gives a qualified `£60 / month` starting point immediately, while this page places its accurate fee table after the intro, services and records sections ([fees](desktop-fees-section.png)). Moving one sourced, qualified price cue or quote factor upward would make the early offer more tangible without inventing proof.
3. **P2: the quote CTA adds an email-app handoff that appears only after entry.** The public site's quote/consultation links are direct `mailto:info@clarentis.co.uk`; here `Request your fixed-fee quote` opens a form with four required fields, then `Prepare email draft` produces a `mailto:` link. The [modal](mobile-modal.png) and [handoff](mobile-email-handoff.png) accurately say the page has not sent anything. The initial CTA does not prepare visitors for that extra step or the need for an email app. A short cue beside the first CTA, or a direct email option near it, would reduce surprise while retaining the source's email conversion type.

## Visual and Source Assessment

- **Responsive hierarchy:** The exact source logo is clear on white. Desktop copy uses the hero image's dark left space without covering the notebook, calculator or receipts. At 390 and 768, copy and image stack cleanly; at 320, the CTA still fits. The assurance strip begins at y=716 on 390x844 and y=609 on 320x700, providing readable continuation. At 1280x600, the hero ends at y=514 and the strip ends at y=595. No horizontal overflow appeared in the six captures. The normal [mobile footer](mobile-footer-normal-scroll.png) and [desktop footer](desktop-footer-normal-scroll.png) keep phone, email and privacy legible.
- **Image integrity:** The hero original retains its notebook/calculator/receipt subject on desktop, tablet and mobile ([desktop](desktop-first.png), [tablet](tablet-first.png), [mobile](mobile-first.png)). The original consultation scene's two faces and checklist remain intact in the [intro](desktop-intro-section.png); the receipt-handling action remains visible in [records](mobile-records-section.png); the blank calendar and folders remain visible in [process](tablet-process-section.png). All four images are identified as illustrative on the page; none is presented as a real Clarentis person, client or document. Their relevance is adequate, though the page still has no first-party team, credential or customer proof. Such proof should only be added if the business supplies verifiable material.
- **Brand and visual age:** The logo and navy/teal palette track the source, while the full-bleed hero, unframed sections and fee table are more current than its card-heavy layout. The palette is conservative but the warm image tones prevent it reading as a single flat blue field. The source and new page both declare an `Inter, ... system-ui` stack; Chrome on this Mac painted both heading and prose in `.SF NS` with no custom font. No formal brand guide was provided, and rendering on other operating systems was not checked.
- **Useful coverage:** All six public services, six published fee ranges and their scope/complexity qualifiers, four onboarding steps, free consultation, email and phone appear in the new page ([services](desktop-services-section.png), [fees](desktop-fees-section.png), [process](desktop-process-section.png)). The FAQ largely reiterates fit, consultation, fees and onboarding rather than resolving a new objection ([FAQ](mobile-faq-section.png)); this is a lesser editorial opportunity. The fee table and footer remain readable on mobile. The public site's `Why Clarentis` language is mostly recast as process and record-keeping copy, without unsourced credentials or testimonials.

## Limits

This pass inspected visual states and local draft wording only. It did not open or send the generated email, submit a real enquiry, publish, test a private account, audit interaction edge cases, or establish image-generation provenance; those are outside this review's assigned scope. The local page showed no missing rendered content images in the inspected placements. The only captured HTTP error was the *public source site's* missing `/favicon.ico`, not a landing-page asset failure. A normal-scrolling recapture removed an early skip-link screenshot artifact. No fixes were made or retested.
