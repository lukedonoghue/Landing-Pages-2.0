# Buyer Psychology

The likely buyer is not choosing a low-cost side business. They are deciding whether a capital-intensive operating platform deserves deeper diligence. Their central job is to reduce four kinds of uncertainty: what the integrated model requires, whether their background transfers, what support replaces independent build-from-scratch work, and what evidence belongs in the FDD rather than marketing copy.

The emotional tension is control versus unsupported complexity. Established dealers want new reach without giving up operating discipline. Leaders entering automotive want a defined system without pretending experience is irrelevant. Multi-unit investors want repeatable infrastructure, but will resist hype about passive income or guaranteed scale.

The page should move from model clarity to operating proof, human proof, fit, investment context, support, process, objections, and a calm next step. The guide earns the lead by helping the buyer prepare better questions; the conversion is not positioned as a commitment to buy.

Key desired outcomes are a clearer model comparison, a realistic view of capitalization and responsibilities, confidence that support covers the major operating functions, and an orderly route to FDD/advisor review.
